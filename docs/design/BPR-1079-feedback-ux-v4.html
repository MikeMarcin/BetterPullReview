The design

MarkUnder the author's avatar, centred on it, for every post, root and reply alike. v3 put a 15 px mark under an 18 px avatar. An odd width can't centre on an even one, so the rendered ink sat 2 px in on the left and 1 px on the right, which is why it read as right-aligned. The v4 mark is 16 px, so the ink sits 1 px in on each side at 1× and 2 px at 2×.
RailA rule down the post's left edge, in the label's tint, with no mark on it. A plain post gets a grey rule. The rail goes in space the card already had: the card's 9 px left padding for the root, and the reply's 24 px indent for a reply. Its right edge is always 3 px left of the avatar. A thicker rail grows outward, so it never moves the text.
WidthNo width is added compared with plain A. Measured in the detail frame at the wide tray (380 px): plain A's root text starts 35 px from the card edge and is 335 px wide, and its replies start at 59 px and are 311 px wide. v4's numbers are the same. v3's D cost about 21 px on every post.
BlockingTwo cues, and neither is text. The mark is filled with its tint and its letters turn white or near-black, whichever contrasts more. A normal mark keeps the soft fill. The rail is 4 px instead of 2 px. Because the cue is the fill rather than the colour, it also shows in greyscale and with colour-vision differences. The lowest letter contrast on a filled mark, across all twelve tints in all four theme variants, is 4.52:1. "(non-blocking)" and undecorated labels look the same.
Accessible nameThe mark is an image named with the label and its decoration, for example "issue (blocking)". The post is an article named with the author and the same words: "tbutler, issue (blocking)". The tooltip shows the same words. These were read from Chrome's accessibility tree.
EditingAs on the accepted composition page, the label is the highlighted words at the start of the field ("suggestion (blocking): "). The mark and rail follow those words.
