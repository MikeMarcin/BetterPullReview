The opened report, in the file tab

The report opens as a file tab. That was decided, and round 2 was wrong to draw it anywhere else. This round keeps the place fixed and asks only how the report reads there: what is folded when it opens, how you move between its sections, and how you get back to the conversation.

Owner, 2026-11-14, rejecting round 2: “Gross, no. They should all be in the file tab that was already decided upon.”

  • Fixed in all three: the report is a tab in the files’ strip, marked with the agent glyph (Q8). The review bar’s file controls are drawn off while it is active (Q9). There is no Ask on the report’s tab. The card is the one round 1 chose, and its attachment row is selected while the report’s tab is the active one.
  • Every frame is the window at its real size (1280 by 820 points; the tab’s pane is 631 points) in the application’s own colour roles.
  • The report is round 2’s long one: a review of #21 with a summary, six sections, four sub-sections, two code blocks, eleven code references and two suggested changes. A suggested change is its lines as a diff with Add as draft comment under it; nothing is published.
  • Each direction has two frames: the tab as it opens, and the tab a moment into reading.

Where it starts: the chosen card

A · Outline: it opens as its headings

What you first see is the shape of the report: the summary, then every heading with its first sentence. No body is open. You open the ones you came for, in place.

What it gives
  • The whole report at first sight, with where the work is.
  • The outline is the document, so it cannot be out of view or out of date.
  • No bar and no side list: the page is all there is.
What it costs
  • Reading from top to bottom takes a press a heading, or Expand all first.
  • Find has to open what it finds in.
  • The body becomes one view for each heading: the largest change to the report’s widget.

B · Straight read: nothing folds

The report is a document and reads like one, from the top. Two quiet things keep your place: a bar at the top that names the section you are in, and a ruler down the right edge with a tick for every heading, the way a scroll bar marks changes in a diff.

What it gives
  • It reads like a document: nothing to press to read on, and Find works as it does anywhere.
  • The ruler shows the report’s shape and your place in it, in sixteen points.
  • The way back is always in the same place.
What it costs
  • A long report is a long scroll, and nothing says what a section holds until you reach it.
  • The bar takes thirty points of the pane.
  • The ruler is a new part to build.

C · One section at a time

A report from a review is a list of things to deal with, so it is read like one: a page for each section, and you step through them. The conversation is the page before the first.

What it gives
  • Each section is one screen with nothing else competing for it.
  • Stepping through is one press, and the page says where it leads.
  • The way back is where back usually is: before the first page.
What it costs
  • A narrative report (Explain this file) is cut into pages it was not written for.
  • A section longer than the pane still scrolls, so there are two ways to move.
  • From a late page the conversation is the menu’s first row, not one press of previous.

A and C open alike

Both first show the summary and a list of sections. They differ in what a press does: in A the section opens where it is and the rest stay around it; in C the section replaces the page. B is the only one that opens on the report’s own words.
