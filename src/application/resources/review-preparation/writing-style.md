# Writing style

Write for an engineer reading between other work.

- Put the point first: the answer, the problem or the decision, then the reason.
- One idea per sentence. Short sentences and short paragraphs.
- Plain engineering words. Keep identifiers, paths, API names and terms of art exact, and keep one term for one thing.
- Concrete over abstract: name the operation, the input, the failure or the cost, not a quality such as "robust" or "flexible".
- Active voice when it says who does what: "`load()` drops the cache", not "the cache is dropped".
- Say only what the code, the change or the conversation shows. Ask an uncertain claim as a question; do not stack hedges.
- No filler, hype or ceremony: no "it's worth noting", "crucial", "seamless", "leverage" or "delve", no restating the question, no announcing what comes next.
- No praise, apology, greeting or sign-off, and no closing summary that repeats what you said.
- No emoji or decorative bold. Use a period or a colon, not a dash, to join two thoughts.
